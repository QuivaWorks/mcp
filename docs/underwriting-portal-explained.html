01Where it stands today

The portal is a working system for underwriting Builders Risk insurance — cover for a building while it is under construction. An underwriter can record who is asking for cover, capture the details of the building project, price it, turn an accepted price into a policy, and produce the paperwork.

Three things are true at once, and it is worth being straight about all three before anything else.

The information and the paperwork work properly

The forms, the workspace, the printable quote and the printable policy schedule are all built, live, and tested. You can open them right now.

The pricing maths is correct, and we proved it

We ran a real example through the system: a $500,000 timber-framed new build, eight months long, a builder with seven years' experience, in a standard urban area. The system produced a premium of $2,012.50 — matching, to the cent, the figure worked out by hand in the original requirements document.

The one significant catch

The automatic steps do not currently start when a person presses Save on a form. This is a fault in the platform itself, not in anything we built — the Save button leaves out a small piece of information that the automation system needs before it will run.

We proved this precisely. We created two identical quotes. One saved the normal way did nothing at all. One saved with that missing piece included priced itself correctly and raised the right follow-up task. One field is the entire difference.

So until a developer fixes that, an underwriter has to do the pricing and the policy creation by hand. Everything needed for it to work automatically is already built and waiting.

One automatic step is unaffected and genuinely runs every day: the renewal check. It works because it runs on a timer rather than reacting to somebody saving a form.

02The five building blocks

Everything in the portal is made from five kinds of thing. The platform has its own names for them, which you will see in the screens, so both names are given here.

In plain words Called internally What it is for
A form that holds information record config Defines what questions get asked and what answers are allowed. Filling one in creates a record — one applicant, one quote, one policy.
A shared workspace space Where the team's work lives. Holds the to-do list and links the relevant forms together in one place.
A job on someone's list task A single piece of work assigned to a person, with a status and a due date. Most are raised automatically.
An automatic helper flow A set of steps the system runs on its own — do the pricing sum, create the policy, check for renewals.
A printable document document template A Word layout with gaps in it. The system fills the gaps from a quote or policy and produces a finished PDF.

There is a sixth, smaller one: an assistant (assistant), a chat helper that answers questions about the pricing rules; and a meeting notes format (meeting template) that structures the write-up of an underwriting meeting.

03What we built

Eleven components in total. Each one below says what it is for, what it is called internally, and where to see it.

Forms that hold the information

Applicant

record config · UWClient Live

The builder, contractor or property owner asking for cover. Their legal business name, address, contact details, licence number, how long they have been trading, and what kind of business they are.

Why it exists
Two of these details change the price. A builder trading under two years pays 25% more; one trading over ten years pays 10% less. So this is not just an address book — it feeds the pricing.
Size
8 questions
See it
Open the Applicant form

Quote

record config · UWQuote Live

The heart of the system. Everything needed to price a job: who is insured, the project itself (what is being built, where, how big, how long, what it is made of), how much cover is wanted, and an assessment of what could go wrong on site. It then holds the price the system works out.

Why it exists
A quote is a real business document with a life of its own — it gets drafted, submitted, priced, then accepted, declined or left to expire. It needs to be a thing you can find, revisit and report on, not a passing conversation.
Clever bit
The form changes as you fill it in. Tick "prior losses" and a box appears asking for details. Choose "Renovation" and it asks about the scope of the renovation. Choose "Demolition" and it asks for the demolition permit instead. Irrelevant questions stay hidden.
Size
47 questions, in six labelled groups
See it
Open the Quote form

Policy

record config · UWPolicy Live

The live insurance contract, created once a customer accepts a quote. Carries the policy number, the dates cover runs between, the premium, and whether it has been paid. It always records which quote it came from.

Why it exists separately
A quote is an offer; a policy is a promise. They need to be kept apart. A quote can be re-priced or withdrawn — a policy, once issued, is a contract, and the paperwork has to reflect what was actually agreed.

29 questions

Where the team works

Underwriting workspace

space · UNDERWRITING Live

The team's shared home. Its to-do list is where every piece of follow-up work appears: review this price, finish setting up that policy, chase these renewals. Jobs move through four states — Open, In Progress, Completed, Cancelled.

Why it exists
Because the automatic helpers need somewhere to put work. The system cannot email anyone or send an alert — creating a job on someone's list is the only way it can get a person's attention. That makes this workspace the portal's inbox.
Also here
All three forms are linked into this workspace, so an underwriter finds applicants, quotes and policies in one place rather than hunting for them.

The automatic helpers

Price the quote

flow · Quote Pricing Built, not triggering

When a quote is marked ready, this works out the premium and writes it back onto the quote, then asks an underwriter to check it.

What it does
Checks the essential details are present. Looks up the base rate for the type of project. Works out the starting premium from the value insured. Applies four adjustments — for how long the job runs, what it is built from, how experienced the builder is, and how risky the location is. Enforces the $500 minimum. Marks the quote "Priced" and raises a review job.
If something is missing
It puts the quote back to Draft and raises a job naming exactly which details are needed, rather than pricing something incomplete.
Why a helper, not a form
A form can hold a number but cannot work one out from other numbers. Any calculation has to be done by a helper like this one.
Status
The maths is correct and proven. It does not start on its own when someone presses Save — see Where it stands today.
See it
Open Quote Pricing
Worth knowing

The rate tables live in the helper as editable settings, not buried in code. The base rates, all four adjustment tables and the minimum premium can be changed without rebuilding anything. It is still a change someone technical makes — but it is a change to a list of numbers, not to a program.

Turn an accepted quote into a policy

flow · Quote Acceptance and Policy Creation Built, not triggering

When a customer accepts, this creates the policy, copies the agreed details across, works out the dates, links the two records together, and asks an underwriter to finish the setup.

What it does
Gives the policy a reference. Sets cover to start on the project start date and run twelve months. Copies the insured, the project and the cover across. Marks the premium as unpaid. Points the quote at its new policy so the trail is intact both ways.
Why it exists
Re-typing agreed figures from a quote onto a policy is exactly where expensive mistakes happen. Copying them mechanically means the policy cannot disagree with what was quoted.
Status
Tested and working end to end — it created a policy, linked it, and raised the setup job. Same triggering limitation as above.
See it
Open Quote Acceptance

Daily renewal check

flow · Daily Renewal Scan Runs daily

Every morning this looks through the live policies for any expiring in about a month, and raises a single job listing them so nobody's renewal is missed.

Why one job, not one per policy
The platform's helpers can read many records but can only write to one place. So a job per expiring policy is not possible. A single summary listing them all is — and it is arguably better, because an underwriter sees the whole week's renewals in one view instead of a scattered pile of reminders.
Status
Genuinely working. We ran it and it correctly found one policy expiring in 29 days and listed it, with its reference, customer, project, date and premium.
When
Every 24 hours, first run 5 August at 07:00 UTC

The paperwork

Builders Risk Quotation

document template · builders_risk_quote Live

The quote a customer actually receives. Six sections: who is insured, the project, the cover, the price with every adjustment shown and explained, the terms, and how to accept — with signature lines.

Why show the workings
A customer who can see that their timber frame cost them more and their ten years' experience saved them money can have a sensible conversation about it. A single unexplained number invites an argument.
Filled-in gaps
28, every one checked against the Quote form
Tested

The two that are built but not yet switched on

Underwriting Assistant

assistant Waiting to be installed

A chat helper for underwriters. It knows every pricing rule by heart and can explain how a premium was arrived at, talk through cover and exclusions, and walk someone through the process.

What it will not do
It cannot look anything up. It cannot fetch a quote or a policy. We built it to say so plainly and ask for the details to be pasted in, rather than inventing a number — a made-up premium in a customer conversation is far worse than no answer.
Also built in
It knows which figures are filled in automatically, so if one is blank it says the automation has not run rather than reading the blank as zero.
Why no link
It is written and ready, but assistants are created at the moment the portal is installed into a company's account. Nothing exists to open until then.

Underwriter Meeting Summary

meeting template Waiting to be installed

A fixed structure for writing up an underwriting meeting: who was there, which risks were discussed, what was decided about pricing, what worried people, what was referred on, and who agreed to do what.

Why it matters
Months later, when a claim comes in, the only record of why a risk was priced or declined the way it was may be these notes. The template forces the reasoning to be written down, not just the outcome.

04How a deal flows through it

This is the whole product in sequence. Each step says who does it, and where the automation would take over if it were triggering.

  1. 1

    A builder asks for cover

    Underwriter · Applicant form

    An underwriter records the business: name, address, contact, licence, years trading. This is now a permanent record everything else can point at.

  2. 2

    The project is written up as a quote

    Underwriter · Quote form

    What is being built, where, how big, how long, what from. How much cover is wanted. What could go wrong on site. The form hides the questions that do not apply. It starts as a Draft, and the underwriter moves it to Submitted when it is complete.

  3. 3

    The premium is worked out

    Automatic helper · Quote Pricing

    Base rate for the project type, multiplied by the value insured, then adjusted four times — duration, construction, builder experience, location. Minimum $500. The price and every adjustment are written back onto the quote, which becomes Priced, and a review job appears on the underwriter's list.

    Manual for now  Until the platform fault is fixed, an underwriter applies the rate tables themselves and types the result in. The Assistant can talk them through it.

  4. 4

    The quote document goes out

    Underwriter · Quotation template

    A PDF is produced from the priced quote, showing the price and how it was reached, with signature lines. It is filed in storage and linked from the quote.

  5. 5

    The customer accepts

    Underwriter · Quote form

    The underwriter sets the quote to Accepted. That single change is the signal for everything in the next step.

  6. 6

    The policy is created

    Automatic helper · Quote Acceptance

    A policy appears, carrying the agreed figures across untouched. It gets a reference, cover dates running twelve months from the project start, and a premium marked unpaid. The quote and policy point at each other. A setup job lands on the underwriter's list, due tomorrow.

    Manual for now  Same fault. The underwriter creates the policy by hand and copies the figures across.

  7. 7

    The policy schedule is issued

    Underwriter · Policy Schedule template

    The formal confirmation of cover, produced from the policy and sent to the customer.

  8. 8

    A month before expiry, the renewal is flagged

    Automatic helper · Daily Renewal Scan

    Every morning the system checks for policies expiring in about a month and raises one job listing them all. The underwriter works down the list, raising a renewal quote for each — which starts this whole sequence again at step 2.

    Working today

How the pieces hold together

The forms hold the facts. The helpers move facts between forms and do the sums forms cannot. The workspace is where helpers ask people for things. The templates turn facts into paperwork a customer can read. And the references — every quote and policy has one — are the thread that lets you follow one job from enquiry to renewal.

05What it deliberately cannot do

The original requirements asked for a number of things the platform genuinely cannot provide. They were left out on purpose rather than half-built, and it is better to know that now than to discover it in a demo.

Asked for Why not
A drag-and-drop board of work No board view exists on the platform. Work shows as a list.
Notifications and emails Nothing can send a message. A job on someone's list is the only way to get their attention — so every "notify the underwriter" step became a job instead.
Reference numbers like BR-2026-00001 The platform has no safe way to count. Two people accepting at the same moment could get the same number. References are built from each record's own unique id instead — guaranteed unique, just not in sequence.
Documents attached to a record Records hold no files. Documents go to file storage and the record holds a link.
Sorting and filtering the lists Table views can choose which columns appear, and nothing more.
One renewal quote raised per expiring policy A helper can read many records but write to one. Hence the single summary list.
Automatic reassignment when a risk is referred to a colleague Same reason — it would mean reassigning several jobs at once.
Reporting, conversion rates, productivity figures These are platform-wide capabilities, not something a product like this can add.
Access control, change history, encryption, uptime guarantees Also platform-wide concerns, outside what this portal configures.

There is a fuller written record of all of this, with the reason for each decision, in the project's own specification document.

07What happens next

Three things are outstanding, in order of importance.

Install it into a real company account

The whole portal is packaged and ready — 11 components sitting in the template library, waiting to be switched on. Doing that needs a company account other than the one it was built in, which we do not currently have access to. Installing it into the account it was built in would prove nothing, because everything is already there.

This is the last unproven step. Everything up to it has been tested.

Get the automation fault fixed

A developer needs to make the Save button include the piece of information the automation system waits for. We have a precise write-up of where and why, with a reproducible test.

One thing to weigh before it is done: that change switches automatic steps on across the whole platform, including for the two products already live with customers, some of them possibly for the first time. It should be tested somewhere safe first.

Have an underwriter use it

Everything reported here was tested by us. The forms have not yet been through a real underwriter pricing a real job, which is the only way to find out whether the questions are the right questions and in the right order.
